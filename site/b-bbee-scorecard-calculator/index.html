How a B-BBEE scorecard works

Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment (B-BBEE) is government's programme to increase the meaningful participation of black people in the South African economy. It is set out in the B-BBEE Act 53 of 2003 (amended by Act 46 of 2013) and measured through the Codes of Good Practice.

1. Find your scorecard

Your annual turnover and sector decide whether you are an Exempted Micro Enterprise (EME), a Qualifying Small Enterprise (QSE) or a Generic (large) entity, and whether a sector code applies.

2. Score each element

Points are earned against targets for ownership, management control, skills development, enterprise and supplier development, and socio-economic development. Points are pro-rated: reach half a target and you earn half the points.

3. Convert points to a level

Your total points give a B-BBEE level from 1 to 8 (or non-compliant). Missing a sub-minimum on a priority element drops you one level. Your level sets the procurement recognition your clients can claim on spend with you.

Why your level matters

  • Clients claim your recognition level in their own preferential procurement score, so a better level makes you a stronger supplier.
  • Government departments and state-owned entities consider B-BBEE status in tenders, licences, concessions and public-private partnerships.
  • Skills Development is a priority element worth 20 points (plus 5 bonus) on the Generic scorecard, and it is one of the most controllable elements.
  • Certificates are valid for 12 months, so your score must be maintained each year.

Priority elements and discounting

Ownership, skills development and enterprise and supplier development are priority elements. Each has a sub-minimum of 40% of its points.

Generic entities must meet the sub-minimum on all three priority elements. QSEs must meet ownership and at least one of skills development or enterprise and supplier development.

If a sub-minimum is missed, the level achieved is discounted by one level (for example, Level 4 becomes Level 5). The discount is applied once, however many sub-minimums are missed.

Example: a large company scoring 82 points (Level 4) that earns only 6 of 20 skills development points drops to Level 5, and its clients' recognition of spend with it falls from 100% to 80%.

SETAs and your scorecard

SETAs (Sector Education and Training Authorities) do not issue B-BBEE scorecards. Sector codes and the Generic Codes do. But your SETA relationship directly affects your skills development points.

Skills levy

Employers with an annual payroll above R500 000 pay a skills development levy of 1% of payroll to SARS, which funds the SETAs and the National Skills Fund.

WSP and ATR

Submitting your Workplace Skills Plan and Annual Training Report (usually by 30 April) unlocks the mandatory grant (20% of levies paid) and is a requirement for claiming skills development points.

Discretionary grants

SETAs fund learnerships, internships, bursaries and other PIVOTAL programmes through discretionary grants, which can reduce your cost of earning skills development points.

Proposed changes in 2026

Draft amendments to the Codes of Good Practice (Gazette 54032) were published for comment on 29 January 2026. They are proposals and are not yet in force.

Transformation Fund option

Large entities could contribute 3% of NPAT (QSEs 2%) to a national Transformation Fund to earn enterprise and supplier development points.

New weightings

The draft increases the total points available on the Generic scorecard, raises bonus points from 9 to 11 and updates preferential procurement targets.

What to do now

Keep measuring on the current Codes. Comments closed on 30 March 2026. We will update this calculator if and when final amendments are gazetted.

Frequently asked questions

Is this calculator result my official B-BBEE level?

No. It is an indicative estimate. Only a SANAS-accredited verification agency can issue a B-BBEE certificate, and qualifying EMEs and QSEs use a sworn affidavit.

Who sees my results?

Your results are shared with the Skills College sales team (sales@skillscollege.co.za) so we can help you understand them. We do not share them with anyone else.

How can Skills College improve my score?

We plan and manage learnerships, internships, bursaries, disability programmes and absorption, which earn skills development points, and we help you spend your skills budget on what counts.

Can training of unemployed people count?

Yes. Since the 2019 amendment, spend on training unemployed black people counts towards the learning programmes indicator, and learners on learnerships count towards the headcount indicator.

What if my sector has its own code?

You must use your sector code. The calculator flags your result so our team can check it against the right scorecard.

How often must I be verified?

B-BBEE certificates and affidavits are valid for 12 months from the date of issue.
